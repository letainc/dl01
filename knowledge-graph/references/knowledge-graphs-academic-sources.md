## Foundations and knowledge graphs

1. Stanford CS520 — Knowledge Graphs (Spring 2021); syllabus, videos and slide links: https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs520/ — entity-relation modeling, construction, inference and application.
2. MIT OCW 6.042J — Mathematics for Computer Science (Fall 2010), Ch. 5 Graph Theory: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-042j-mathematics-for-computer-science-fall-2010/resources/mit6_042jf10_chap05/ — graphs before learning algorithms.
3. Hogan et al. (2021), `Knowledge Graphs`, open academic text: https://kgbook.org/ — precise models, semantics, access, quality and construction.
4. W3C RDF 1.1 Concepts: https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf11-concepts/ — normative model for RDF statements. 
5. W3C SPARQL 1.1 Query Language: https://www.w3.org/TR/sparql11-query/ — standard graph pattern queries.
6. W3C OWL 2 Overview: https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-overview/ — ontology, logical semantics and inference; later stage.

## Critical reading questions for every source

- What data model, relation semantics, and identity assumptions does it require?
- Is it a knowledge representation technique, a graph database, a deterministic algorithm, or a learned model?
- What is the training target, baseline, split strategy and leakage risk, if ML?
- Is this a research prototype or maintained production technology?
- What fails with missing/mistyped/reversed/stale edges, heterogeneity, temporal updates and high-degree hubs?
- How does the idea extend rather than overwrite existing relational Feature Engineering concepts?
